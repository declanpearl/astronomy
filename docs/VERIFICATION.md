## Batch 4: checked against published papers/reviews (search results read during development)
- RR Lyrae periods about 0.2-0.9 day (arXiv:1707.03954); RR Lyrae are old-population stars in halos and globular clusters (arXiv:1001.1648); visual absolute magnitude is nearly constant on the horizontal branch and depends on metallicity, e.g. M_V = 0.23[Fe/H] + 0.93 in one review (arXiv:astro-ph/0301550); a strong period-luminosity relation appears in the near-infrared (arXiv:2305.19437).
- Type Ia: slower-declining supernovae are more luminous (Phillips relation); typical decline parameter dm15(B) near 1.1 mag, range roughly 0.7-1.9 (arXiv:1205.6835). The form M_B,max = -21.726 + 2.698 dm15(B) is quoted from Phillips (1993) in arXiv:1809.04479; used in q115 as a supplied relation, not as a recommended calibration.
- Cepheid period-luminosity relation: a straight line in log P, standardizable candles (arXiv:1205.6835).

## Batch 5: Kepler's laws, rotation curves (sources read during development; not peer review of this app)
- Kepler's three laws as stated here (ellipse with the Sun at one focus; equal areas in equal times, so faster at perihelion; P² proportional to a³, and P² = a³ with years and AU): consistent with the University of Nebraska NAAP page https://cse.unl.edu/~astro/naap/pos/pos_background1.html and Astronomy magazine's explainer https://www.astronomy.com/science/astronomy-101-keplers-laws/.
- Equal-area law as a consequence of angular momentum conservation, and Newton's derivation of all three laws from an inverse-square force: stated in https://synapse.jtauber.com/node/keplers-laws/ (early-draft educational site; treat as supporting only). The v_peri/v_apo = r_apo/r_peri relation follows from angular momentum conservation at the apsides (derivable; standard).
- Rotation curves of spiral galaxies are flat rather than Keplerian in the outer regions, which implied large amounts of unseen (dark) matter: Physics Today letter https://physicstoday.aip.org/letters/explaining-a-few-discoveries ; historical context at https://astrobites.org/2016/12/27/how-one-person-discovered-the-majority-of-the-universe-the-work-of-vera-rubin/ . Estimates of the dark-to-luminous ratio differ between sources (roughly ten to thirty times in different accounts), so no ratio is asserted in the app.

## Batch 6: galaxies, populations, interactions (sources read during development; mostly NASA/ESA/STScI press-level text plus one arXiv paper)
- **Antennae (NGC 4038/4039):** two spiral galaxies that began interacting a few hundred million years ago; tidal tails formed in the initial encounter 200-300 million years ago; super star clusters, about 10% expected to survive beyond 10 Myr, roughly a hundred of the most massive possibly becoming globular clusters; very high star-formation rate described as a starburst. Sources: https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/the-antennae-galaxiesngc-4038-4039 , https://science.nasa.gov/image-detail/antennae-galaxies-reloaded/ , https://www.universetoday.com/articles/colliding-spiral-galaxies-captured-by-hubble . Distance: sources disagree (about 45 million ly from Hubble red-giant data in the Universe Today article versus about 60 million ly in an APOD text); the app states the disagreement rather than a single value.
- **Arp 147:** relatively undisturbed galaxy at left with a smooth ring of starlight seen nearly edge-on; clumpy blue star-forming ring at right, "most probably" formed after the left galaxy passed through; Cetus; more than 400 million ly; ring about 30,000 ly across (APOD). Sources: https://www.astropix.org/image/stsci/2008-37a , https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2008-november-4-the-double-ring-galaxies-of-arp-147-from-hubble/ . The collision origin is an interpretation.
- **NGC 1569:** dwarf irregular starburst; tip-of-the-red-giant-branch distance 3.36 +/- 0.20 Mpc versus an earlier assumed 2.2 +/- 0.6 Mpc; member of the IC 342 group; three super star clusters; the paper says starbursts are often triggered by interactions and prior star-formation-rate estimates rise by more than a factor of 2. ESA/Hubble: about 11 million ly, stars made over 100 times faster than the Milky Way for almost 100 Myr, interactions suspected to compress gas. Sources: https://www.arxiv.org/abs/0808.0153 , https://esahubble.org/images/potw1626a/ . The older APOD value (7 million ly) is superseded.
- **Galaxy collisions:** stars very rarely collide, gas clouds do and trigger star formation (APOD text on the Antennae, and APOD on Arp 147).
- Earlier verified entries (MCG+07-33-027, Arp 143, Cartwheel) are unchanged and are used in Batch 6 questions only through supplied excerpts.

## Batch 7: compact objects, ULXs, SN Ia physics, gravitational waves, multi-messenger (primary papers and institutional pages read during development)
- **M82 X-2:** Bachetti et al. 2014, Nature 514, 202 (https://sites.astro.caltech.edu/~srk/XC/Notes/Bachetti_ULX.pdf): NuSTAR coherent pulsations, mean period 1.37 s, sinusoidal modulation 2.53 d, linear spin-up (period derivative about -2e-10 s/s), accreting magnetized neutron star; discovered while observing SN 2014J (NASA/Texas Tech text). Luminosity about 1.8e40 erg/s and "more than 100 times" the Eddington limit of a 1.4 solar-mass neutron star: a review, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10537 ; the discovery paper's pulsed peak luminosity is quoted as about 4.9e39 erg/s, so the factor depends on whether pulsed or total luminosity is used, and q-level wording states the review value.
- **M82 X-1:** Pasham, Strohmayer, Mushotzky 2014, Nature (arXiv:1501.03180, https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.03180): twin-peak QPOs 3.32 +/- 0.06 and 5.07 +/- 0.06 Hz (3:2), mass 428 +/- 105 Msun (inverse mass scaling) and 415 +/- 63 Msun (relativistic precession model); earlier estimates from about 20 to over 1000 Msun. The mass is model-dependent (extends a stellar-mass relation). Press coverage (Physics World) describes it as a confirmed candidate.
- **SN 2014J:** Type Ia in M82, discovered 2014 Jan 21.8 UT (Fossey et al.); closest SN Ia in at least 28 years; M82 distance 3.3 Mpc (Foley et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.3677) versus 3.77 +/- 0.66 Mpc (NED mean quoted in https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.7066); peak V = 10.6; A_V = 2.0 +/- 0.1 inferred assuming a typical SN Ia luminosity; reddening plus circumstellar scattering. Two progenitor scenarios (single-degenerate WD plus non-degenerate companion; double-degenerate WD merger) are described in arXiv:1409.7066.
- **GW170817:** Abbott et al. 2017, ApJ Letters 848, L12, "Multi-messenger Observations of a Binary Neutron Star Merger" (LIGO DCC https://dcc.ligo.org/P1700294/public , PoS summary https://pos.sissa.it/331/013 ): merger time 12:41:04 UTC on 2017 Aug 17; GRB 170817A about 1.7 s later (Fermi GBM; INTEGRAL also); 31 deg2 at 40 +/- 8 Mpc; component masses 0.86-2.26 Msun (the Phys. Rev. Lett. paper quotes 1.17-1.60 under a low-spin prior); optical transient AT 2017gfo / SSS17a in NGC 4993 less than 11 h later; X-ray about 9 d and radio about 16 d; kilonova powered by radioactive decay of r-process nuclei (stated as supported by the observations).
- **LIGO basics:** 4 km arms; strain h = dL/L; typical strain about 1e-21 and arm change about 1e-18 to 1e-17 m (LIGO/Caltech and AIP Physics Today material, e.g. https://aip.brightspotcdn.com/PTO.v69.i4.14_1.online.pdf , https://dcc-lho.ligo.org/public/0036/G050629/000/G050629-00.pdf ); GW frequency is twice the orbital frequency for a binary (LIGO Caltech slides).
- Magnetic-field, characteristic-age and ULX threshold conventions were NOT searched: see below.
